# Houston café permits: full research report (2026-09-23)

Research-agent report covering entity/tax registrations, site, building, health, fire, signs, sidewalk, alarm, TABC and filing order. Complements NOTES.md and houston-requirements.json. Items marked "not verified" need agency confirmation.


**Scope:** espresso, pastries, possibly light food prep, about 30–60 seats, maybe a patio, no alcohol unless TABC is added.

## Four findings that change your list

1. **Houston's food ordinance was repealed on Sept 1, 2025.** Senate Bill 1008 made Houston adopt the FDA Food Code and the Texas Food Establishment Rules (TFER) by reference and repeal City Code Chapter 20. Permit fees now follow the state's risk-based model instead of employee counts. The fat, oils and grease (FOG) generator permit is the one local permit that survives. Older guides and the city-wide fee schedule still show the old employee-based fees.
2. **Food handlers must be trained within 30 days of hire, not 60.** That is the current rule (25 TAC §228.31(d)) and DSHS says the same.
3. **The separate City of Houston food manager certificate appears to be gone.** A national certified food protection manager (CFPM) exam certificate is enough. One Houston Permitting Center page (HHD1005) still lists the old city certificate. I flag this conflict in section 13.
4. **Houston has no general business license.** The city's Office of Business Opportunity names only four basics: name registration, sales tax permit, EIN and property tax rendition.

**Codes in force:** the 2021 Houston Construction Code took effect Jan 1, 2024. It is based on the 2021 IBC (building), IFC (fire), UPC (plumbing) and UMC (mechanical), with Houston amendments. Houston uses the Uniform Mechanical Code, not the IMC. [HPC code amendments](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/construction-code-modernization/code-amendment), [Houston UMC 2021](https://up.codes/viewer/houston/umc-2021)

## A. Entity, tax and employer registrations (state, federal, county)

### 1. Certificate of Formation for an LLC (Texas SOS Form 205)
- **Agency:** Texas Secretary of State, Business & Public Filings Division. File online through SOSDirect or by mail.
- **Status:** Optional but typical. It is required only if you want an LLC.
- **Data fields:** entity name, registered agent name and address, management structure, organizer signature.
- **Fee:** $300, plus a card processing fee online. Expedited handling is about $25 extra.
- **Processing:** third-party sources give 5–7 business days, and some say 10–12 through SOSDirect. **Not verified** against the SOS's own processing-time page.
- **Validity:** perpetual.
- **Order:** file first. The SOS file number is needed for the sales tax permit and for TABC.
- **Sources:** [Form 205 PDF](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/corp/forms/205_boc.pdf), [fee summary (third party)](https://bizee.com/llc/texas/filing-fees-requirements)

### 2. Assumed Name (DBA)
- **Status:** Conditional. It is needed only if you trade under a name other than the legal entity name.
- **If you form an LLC or other entity:** file SOS **Form 503** only.
  - Since HB 3609 (effective Sept 1, 2019), entities no longer also file with the county.
  - Fee is $25. Validity is up to 10 years, renewable within 6 months before expiry.
  - Fields: assumed name, legal name, entity type and file number, jurisdiction, principal office, duration, Texas counties where the name is used. [SOS Form 503 instructions](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/corp/instructions/503.shtml)
- **If you are a sole proprietor or general partnership:** file with the **Harris County Clerk**.
  - Fee is $24 notarized or $25 non-notarized (plus $1 witnessing fee), plus $0.50 per extra owner. Veterans get a $2 discount.
  - File in person or by mail. No online filing is shown. Valid up to 10 years. [Harris County Clerk](https://www.cclerk.hctx.net/PersonalRecords.aspx)
- **Order:** after formation and before opening bank accounts or signing the lease in the trade name.

### 3. Federal Employer Identification Number (EIN)
- **Agency:** IRS. Apply online (Form SS-4 is the paper route).
- **Status:** Required for an LLC with employees, and in practice for any employer.
- **Fee:** free. Beware of sites that charge for it.
- **Processing:** issued immediately online. You need the responsible party's SSN or ITIN, and must finish in one session.
- **Online hours (ET):** Mon–Fri 6 a.m.–1 a.m.; Sat 6 a.m.–9 p.m.; Sun 6 p.m.–midnight.
- **Order:** right after the SOS filing. The Comptroller, TWC, TABC and payroll all need it.
- **Source:** [IRS – Get an EIN](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number)

### 4. Texas Sales and Use Tax Permit (Comptroller)
- **Agency:** Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts. Apply online through eSystems (the Texas Online Tax Registration Application). Form **AP-201** is the paper or email route, used when an owner or officer has no SSN.
- **Status:** Required. Coffee drinks and pastries sold as prepared food are taxable. The Houston combined rate is up to 8.25%.
- **Data needed:** owners' and officers' SSNs, FEIN, SOS file number, NAICS code, location and start date.
- **Fee:** $0. The Comptroller may require a security bond at its discretion.
- **Processing:** "Allow 2–3 weeks."
- **Validity:** does not expire while you stay active.
- **Order:** after the SOS filing and EIN, and before the first sale. TABC also requires it.
- **Sources:** [Comptroller permit page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/permit/), [Sales tax FAQ](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/sales/faq/permit.php)

### 5. Texas Franchise Tax (added item)
- **Agency:** Texas Comptroller.
- **Status:** Required annual filing for an LLC.
- **Due:** May 15 each year.
- **What to file:** the no-tax-due threshold for 2026 reports is $2.65M, and the No Tax Due Report form is not available for 2026. Entities under the threshold still file a Public Information Report or Ownership Information Report.
- **Order:** the first report is due May 15 of the year after formation.
- **Source:** [Comptroller franchise tax](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/)

### 6. TWC employer (unemployment tax) account
- **Agency:** Texas Workforce Commission. Register online through the free Unemployment Tax Registration (UTR) service, which takes about 20 minutes.
- **Status:** Required once liable. Liability usually starts when you pay $1,500 or more in wages in a quarter, or employ someone in 20 weeks of a year.
- **Deadline:** register within **10 days** of becoming liable. If liable, you get the account number immediately, and an Employer Liability Notice follows by mail.
- **After that:** file quarterly wage reports.
- **Sources:** [TWC register](https://www.twc.texas.gov/services/register-tax), [TWC liable employer](https://www.twc.texas.gov/programs/unemployment-tax/responsibilities-liable-employer)

### 7. New-hire reporting (added item)
- **Agency:** Texas Office of the Attorney General, Child Support Division, at employer.oag.texas.gov.
- **Status:** Required.
- **Deadline:** within 20 calendar days of each hire.
- **Fields:** employer name, address and FEIN; employee name, address, SSN and hire date.
- **Penalty:** $25 per unreported employee.
- **Source:** [OAG New Hire Reporting](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/child-support/employers/new-hire-reporting)

### 8. HCAD business personal property rendition
- **Agency:** Harris Central Appraisal District. File **Form 22.15** by paper, mail or iFile. Fax and email are not accepted.
- **Status:** Conditional, because HB 9 changed the rule from Jan 1, 2026.
  - Required if the personal property at your location is worth **more than $125,000**.
  - At or below $125,000, you may file one rendition certifying that. It stays in effect until the value exceeds $125K or the chief appraiser asks.
  - For a new shop with a sub-$125K equipment package, I recommend filing the one-time certification in your first year. I could not confirm whether the exemption is automatic without it.
- **Deadline:** April 15, following Jan 1 ownership. You can get an automatic extension to May 15, plus 15 more days for good cause.
- **Fields:** property description, quantity, and either a good-faith market value or historical cost with acquisition date.
- **Penalty:** 10% for late filing.
- **Sources:** [HCAD rendition guide](https://hcad.org/hcad-resources/hcad-industrial-personal-property/hcad-guide-to-personal-property-renditions), [OBO four requirements](https://www.houstontx.gov/obo/solutionscenter/four_main_business_requirements.html)

### 9. City of Houston general business license
- **Status:** Not required. Houston regulates by activity (food permit, fire permits, and so on). The city's Office of Business Opportunity names only the four items above.
- **Source:** [OBO Four Main Business Requirements](https://www.houstontx.gov/obo/solutionscenter/four_main_business_requirements.html)

## B. Site due diligence (before signing the lease)

### 10. Deed restrictions, zoning and site plan
- **No zoning:** Houston has no use zoning, but private **deed restrictions** are enforced.
- **What you file:** every building permit for new construction, a change of use, or a remodel needs a signed **Declaration in Support of Application for City of Houston Building Permit**. Use **CE-1380** for an individual owner or **CE-1381** for a business-entity owner.
  - It is signed under penalty of perjury by the **property owner** and checked against HCAD records. Upload proof of ownership if they don't match.
  - It is **not required** for an interior remodel that keeps the same use and occupancy classification.
- **Site plan:** Planning & Development requires a site plan for projects that change use or occupancy group. It shows property lines, easements, setbacks, buildings and building footprint with distances to property lines.
- **Also check** whether the site is in a **historic district** (Certificate of Appropriateness), the **floodplain** (elevation certificate) or a **Special Parking Area**.
- **Sources:** [CE-1105 Commercial Prerequisite Checklist](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/media/1811/download), [Commercial Remodel Permit HPWCODE1150](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/hpwcode1150), [CE-1380 form](https://www.pdinet.pd.houstontx.gov/ilms_online_permits/forms/dr_declaration_by_individual.pdf)

### 11. Off-street parking (City Code Chapter 26, Article VIII)
- **Status:** Conditional. A parking review is required for new construction, additions or a **change of use classification**, so retail to restaurant triggers it.
- **Ratio:** restaurants, including patios and outdoor seating, need **8.0 spaces per 1,000 sq ft** of gross floor area. Retail and shopping centers use a lower ratio (shopping centers 4.0 per 1,000), so a change of use can create a shortfall.
- **Ways to reduce it:**
  - Market-Based Parking areas (Sec. 26-471: CBD, East Downtown, parts of Midtown) get a 100% reduction.
  - Transit-oriented streets (Sec. 26-503) get a 100% or 50% reduction.
  - 10% bicycle reduction (Sec. 26-497).
  - Historic building reduction (Sec. 26-498).
  - Up to 5 spaces by application (Sec. 26-504).
  - Shared parking (26-500) and off-site parking (26-499).
  - A variance through the Planning Commission (26-561).
  - Sec. 26-492 lets a restaurant patio be added without extra parking. I could not read the full condition.
- **Sources:** [Parking handout](https://www.houstontx.gov/planning/docs_pdfs/Parking-Requirements-20210604.pdf), [Planning off-street parking page](https://www.houstontx.gov/planning/Off-Street-Parking-Ordinance.html), [Municode Ch. 26](https://library.municode.com/tx/houston/codes/code_of_ordinances/178617?nodeId=COOR_CH26PA)
- **Not fully verified:** the exact current Sec. 26-492 text (the official code page timed out), and whether any 2025–26 parking amendments passed.

### 12. Wastewater/Water Capacity Reservation (WCR) letter and impact fees
- **Agency:** Houston Public Works, Utility Analysis (WCR) and Impact Fee Administration. Apply in iPermits.
- **Status:** Required for converting a development, increasing occupancy, or remodeling a commercial development. The permit checklist asks for water and sewer availability letters for any change in occupancy.
- **Documents:** recorded deed or HCAD printout, survey or site plan, Applicant Authorization Form UA002, and Tenant Listing Form UA010 if in a shopping center.
- **Fees:**
  - WCR application fee is $66.21 for the first acre, plus $33.10 per extra acre (effective Jan 1, 2025).
  - Impact fees are **$1,830.24 per wastewater service unit** plus a $33.56 admin fee, and about **$2,533 per water service unit**. HPC pages show both $2,533.11 and $2,553.11.
  - One service unit is 250 gallons/day. Credit may be given for the prior use in a conversion.
  - Impact fees are **non-refundable**.
- **Processing:** 7 business days, or 1–3 expedited.
- **Validity:** an unpaid letter lasts 6 months. A paid wastewater reservation lasts until the capacity is used. A paid water reservation lasts 2 years to start construction.
- **Order:** submit the WCR application or letter **with the building plans**. Pay the impact fee **before the 2nd plan review cycle** to stay on the 30-day review goal.
- **Sources:** [WCR Wastewater HPWUA1001](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/hpwua1001), [Impact Fee Administration](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/infrastructure-development-services/impact-fee-administration), [WCR fee notice](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/news-events/water-and-wastewater-capacity-reservation-wcr-letter-fee-increase-notice), [Plan Review page](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/building-code-enforcement/plan-review)
- **Not verified:** the service-unit count for a coffee shop (per seat or per sq ft). The equivalency table PDF would not extract. It is at [media/2326](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/media/2326/download?inline).

**Also call first:** the Health Department says to call **before you buy or lease** (832-393-5100). They will review the prior food establishment's file or inspect it.

## C. Building permit, plan reviews and trade permits

### 13. Commercial remodel / build-out permit (Houston Permitting Center)
- **Agency:** Houston Public Works, Building Code Enforcement, Commercial Plan Review. Address: 1002 Washington Ave. Email: commercial.planreview@houstontx.gov. Phone: 832-394-8810.
- **Portals:** apply and pay in iPermits; upload plans in ProjectDox.
- **Status:** Required for any build-out. A change of occupancy **requires approved plans and permits** to get the new certificate of occupancy (CO).
- **Forms:**
  - **CE-1263** Building Permit Application.
  - **CE-1380/CE-1381** declaration.
  - **CE-1105** Commercial Prerequisite Checklist, uploaded to ProjectDox.
  - **COMcheck** energy report.
- **Required reviews:** CE-1105 lists possible review groups: Planning, Utility Analysis, Utility Infrastructure, Storm Drainage, Floodplain, **Traffic**, **Health**, **Fire Marshal**, Structural, Mechanical, Plumbing, Electrical. For a food and drink project, the Health review is required.
- **Drawings required by CE-1105:**
  - Code analysis sheet with occupancy classification, construction type and fire protection.
  - Room-by-room use labels.
  - Door, hardware, glazing and wall schedules.
  - Every sheet **sealed, signed and dated** where the Texas Architecture/Engineering Practice Acts require. Plans may not be marked "Preliminary."
  - **Mechanical plans** when occupancy changes or equipment or ducts change.
  - **Electrical:** one-line diagram, load analysis and panel schedules when occupancy changes or loads are added.
  - **Plumbing:** riser diagram and floor plan when fixtures are added or use changes.
  - **Traffic/civil plans** for a change in use. An Access Management Form may be required.
  - **Health plans:** a complete equipment plan with manufacturer specs.
  - **Asbestos survey** for work on an existing building.
  - **TDLR EAB (accessibility) project number** if the project costs over $50,000.
  - Fire alarm, sprinkler and signs are **permitted separately**.
- **Fees (2026 schedule):**
  - Building permit fee is based on the valuation of all work, including labor and design. For example, $47 plus $5.36 per $1,000 from $7,001 to $150,000; about $815 at $150,000; about $1,067 at $200,000.
  - Plan review fee is **25% of the estimated permit fee**, non-refundable.
  - Admin fee is $33.56, and the minimum permit is $91.06.
  - Example: a $150K build-out comes to roughly $815 permit + $204 plan review + admin (**approximate**).
- **Review time:** HPC aims for a 30-day permit goal. The first review cycle takes **2–37 business days**. Return corrections within 7 business days to stay on track. The permit page lists 15 business days.
- **Validity:** 6 months, per HPC. Keep inspections going so it doesn't lapse.
- **Small jobs:** a One-Stop walk-in or online path exists for small commercial remodels (walk-in under 2,500 sq ft), but it excludes projects that need MEP reviews. A coffee shop change of use usually won't qualify.
- **Inspections:** structural, electrical, plumbing and mechanical at each stage, then finals.
- **Sources:** [HPWCODE1150](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/hpwcode1150), [CE-1105](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/media/1811/download), [2026 Fee Schedule](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/media/2636/download), [Plan Review](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/building-code-enforcement/plan-review)

### 14. Trade permits: electrical, plumbing, mechanical, gas
- **Status:** Required. Each is pulled by a **Houston-registered licensed contractor** under the building permit.
- **Fees (2026 schedule, examples):**
  - Plumbing minimum $97.56; fixtures $34.24 for 1–3 plus $11.41 each extra; gas $45.53 for up to 4 openings.
  - HVAC is 2% of valuation plus $47.
  - Electrical minimum $91.06, plus per-item charges.
- **Source:** [2026 Fee Schedule](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/media/2636/download)

### 15. Health Department food establishment plan review
- **Agency:** Houston Health Department (HHD), Consumer Health Services, Plan Review. Staff sit at HPC, 1002 Washington Ave., 3rd floor, 832-394-8848. Submittal is through ProjectDox as part of the building permit.
- **Status:** Required for new construction, remodeling a space that was **not** a food establishment, and room additions or equipment changes. It may not be required when you take over a permitted food business unchanged; the inspector decides.
- **Documents (HHD checklist and 2025 HPC workshop):**
  - Scaled floor plan with equipment layout.
  - **Equipment schedule** with manufacturer, model and NSF/UL certifications; spec sheets.
  - Elevations of food equipment and plumbing fixtures.
  - Room **finish schedule**: materials and colors, light-colored surfaces in prep areas.
  - **Menu** and food-flow narrative: receiving, storage, prep, service.
  - **Plumbing plan:** hand sinks, 3-compartment sink, mop sink, floor drains, grease interceptor.
  - Mechanical/ventilation plan.
  - For a remodel: existing, demolition and proposed plans.
  - HACCP plan or variance if you use reduced-oxygen packaging, sous vide, time as a control, and similar methods.
- **Fee:** **not verified** for a fixed establishment. $46.98 is the mobile-unit fee only.
- **Timing:** inside the building permit review cycle. Health approval is part of what's needed to issue the building permit, because Health is one of the required review groups.
- **Note:** HHD's checklist PDF still cites the repealed Chapter 20. The 2025 workshop says TFER and FDA Food Code definitions now govern; for example, the 40% light-reflectance rule and the toilet vestibule rule were dropped.
- **Sources:** [HHD Opening a Food Establishment](https://www.houstonhealth.org/services/permits/food-permits/opening-food-establishment), [HHD Plan Review Checklist](https://www.houstonhealth.org/media/36/download), [HPC 2025 Health workshop](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/sites/g/files/nwywnm431/files/2025-11/New%20-%20Session%204%20-%20Rm%20CD%20-%20Plan%20Review%20Process%20For%20Food%20Establishments%20and%20Pools%20-%20Health.pdf)

### 16. Fire Marshal plan review, fire alarm, sprinkler and kitchen hood suppression
- **Agency:** Houston Fire Department, Fire Marshal's Office. Plan review is at HPC (basement). The Fire Permits Office is on the 2nd floor, 832-394-8811, hfd.permitoffice@houstontx.gov.
- **Plan review:** part of the building permit review (the Fire Marshal review group).
- **Fire alarm and sprinkler:** separate permits.
  - Fire alarm permit: $91.06 for 0–10 devices, $130.08 for 11–25.
  - Sprinkler: $97.56 per valve-controlled group, plus $4.55 per extra head. Sprinkler plan review is $2.28 per head (minimum $91.06).
- **Type I hood fire suppression permit:** $97.56 per system.
- **When sprinklers or alarms are triggered:** these are model-code thresholds from the 2021 IBC, **not** checked against Houston amendments.
  - A-2 (restaurant) sprinklers are required when the fire area exceeds 5,000 sq ft, the occupant load is 100 or more, or the space is not on the exit-discharge level.
  - A manual fire alarm is generally required when the Group A occupant load is 300 or more.
  - A 30–60-seat shop under 5,000 sq ft usually triggers neither, **unless the building already has systems.** Houston requires existing alarm and sprinkler systems to be modified under permit.
- **Is a Type I hood needed?**
  - Houston's mechanical code (2021 UMC) requires a Type I hood over appliances that produce **grease or smoke**.
  - Espresso machines, brewers, and reheating pastries in an electric convection oven generally do **not** need one.
  - Panini presses, griddles, fryers and ovens used for grease-producing cooking typically do. Electric appliances listed to **UL 710B** (ventless) can avoid a Type I hood.
  - A Type II hood may be needed over a hot-water sanitizing dishwasher. HHD's older checklist required one.
  - This is **not** verified against Houston's UMC amendments. Confirm with the Mechanical plan reviewer, using your equipment spec sheets.
- **Sources:** [2026 Fee Schedule](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/media/2636/download), [Fire Marshal Office](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/fire-marshal-office), [Fire Permits Office](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/fire-permits-office), [HFD Forms & Permits](https://houstontx.gov/fire/formsandpermits/), [HHD checklist](https://www.houstonhealth.org/media/36/download)

### 17. Accessibility registration (TDLR, added item)
- **Agency:** Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, Architectural Barriers program. Register online in TABS.
- **Status:** Conditional. Registration is required if the project costs **$50,000 or more**. The EAB project number goes on the Houston permit submittal. Smaller projects still must meet the Texas Accessibility Standards (TAS).
- **Fee:** $175, or $300 if you register after construction is complete.
- **Extra cost:** a Registered Accessibility Specialist (RAS) does the plan review and inspection for a private fee.
- **Order:** register **before construction**. The RAS inspection happens after completion.
- **Sources:** [TDLR AB fees](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ab/abfees.htm), [TDLR AB FAQ](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ab/abfaq.htm)

### 18. Grease interceptor (construction) and FOG Generator Permit (operation)
- **Construction requirement:** City Code Sec. 47-513 requires restaurants and other places where FOG may be generated to "design, install, operate and maintain" an interceptor that meets the Houston Plumbing Code, **with a sample well**. This applies to building permits submitted after Aug 31, 2006.
  - It is designed and permitted through the plumbing plans (UPC Chapter 10 plus Houston amendments). Gravity interceptors are typically 300–500 gallons or more.
  - Coffee shops generate milk fats, so plan on one. Ask plumbing review whether an existing interceptor can be reused.
- **Operating permit:** the FOG Generator Registration (HHD1017) from the HHD Special Waste Program, 832-393-5740.
  - Fee: $123.54 plus $33.56 admin; renewal $123.54.
  - Valid 1 year from the **food dealer permit issue date**, and renewed annually.
  - Apply at 8000 N. Stadium Dr. with the **Affidavit of Holding Capacity** and your food dealer account number.
- **Operating rules:**
  - Pump the interceptor out fully **at least every 90 days** unless a Notice of Waiver is approved.
  - Keep waste manifests on site for **5 years**. Return the generator copy within 15 days.
  - Unannounced inspections with sampling. Fines run $250–$2,000 per violation.
- **Sources:** [HHD1017](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/hhd1017), [HHD Special Waste Generators](https://www.houstonconsumer.org/services/permits/special-waste-permits/special-waste-generators), [HHD checklist item 17](https://www.houstonhealth.org/media/36/download)

### 19. Backflow prevention (espresso machines and the premises)
- **Agency:** Houston Public Works, Cross Connection Control Program. 611 Walker St., 832-395-3800.
- **Status:** Required where a cross-connection exists.
- **Testing:** testable assemblies must be tested **at installation, repair, relocation and annually after**. The tester must be TCEQ-licensed and on Houston's approved company list. Reports use TCEQ-20700. Factory tests are not accepted.
- **Penalty:** fines up to $2,000 per day and possible water shutoff.
- **Espresso machines:** under the UPC, beverage dispensers and carbonators generally need an air gap or an ASSE 1022 vented backflow preventer at the point of use. The machine manufacturer usually specifies this too. The 1022 device is **not** a field-testable assembly, so annual testing usually applies to any **premises or containment** assembly the utility or plumbing review requires. I could not confirm the exact UPC section or a Houston rule specific to espresso machines. Show it on the plumbing plans.
- **Sources:** [HPW Cross Connection Control Program](https://www.houstonpublicworks.org/cross-connection-control-program), [TCEQ-20700](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/goto/20700)

### 20. Certificate of Occupancy (CO)
- **Agency:** Houston Public Works, Building Code Enforcement, Occupancy Inspections. 832-394-8880, occupancy.inspections@houstontx.gov.
- **Status:** Required before any commercial lease space is occupied.
- **When a new CO is needed:** when square footage, occupant load or **occupancy use** changes.
  - **Retail to coffee shop is a change of occupancy**: Mercantile (M) becomes Business (B) if the occupant load is under 50, or Assembly A-2 at 50 or more. So you need a new CO through the permit.
  - Taking over an existing café with no changes may only need a duplicate or name-change certificate ($94).
- **Process:**
  - With a construction permit, the CO follows the passed finals for all trades.
  - Without construction, use Form **CE-1045A** (Occupancy Compliance Inspection) by email. It asks for business name, address, cell phone, square footage, floors, and current and previous business type. You pay within 5 days of getting the link.
  - COs are now emailed with a QR code.
- **Fees (2026):** CO $94 (for spaces under 3,000 sq ft in multi-tenant buildings done with the core inspection). A CO or Life Safety inspection is $537.12 for the first story. A retail or office space under 3,000 sq ft done separately is $268.56. Temporary CO is $94.
- **Validity:** indefinite unless the use, occupant load or area changes.
- **Sources:** [HPWCODE1106](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/hpwcode1106), [Occupancy Inspections](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/building-code-enforcement/occupancy-inspections), [CO How-To](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/media/6036/download), [2026 Fee Schedule](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/media/2636/download)

### 21. Place of Assembly permit (occupant load of 50 or more)
- **Agency:** HFD Fire Permits Office. This is an operational permit under Houston Fire Code Sec. 105.
- **Status:** Conditional. The city fee schedule's tiers start at **50 occupants** ("Places of assembly 50–100 occupants, includes partial cost of Life Safety Inspection").
  - Occupant load uses about 15 net sq ft per person for tables and chairs, plus standing and queue areas and possibly the patio.
  - So a 60-seat shop likely crosses 50, and a 30-seat shop likely does not.
  - Crossing 50 also means A-2 classification, **two exits**, and **panic hardware** (model IBC, not checked against Houston amendments), plus a posted occupant-load sign.
- **Fee:** $268.56 for 50–100 occupants; $402.84 for 101–299.
- **Validity:** 1 year, non-transferable. Renew annually or when egress paths change.
- **Order:** after the CO and before opening. It includes a life-safety inspection.
- **Related:** open-flame or candle permits in assembly areas are separate.
- **Sources:** [City fee schedule](https://cohweb.houstontx.gov/FIN_FeeSchedule/default.aspx), [HFD Forms & Permits](https://houstontx.gov/fire/formsandpermits/), [Houston IFC amendments](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/media/9051/download)
- **Not verified:** the exact threshold wording in Houston IFC 105.5 (ICC site returned 403). The 50-person threshold is inferred from the fee tiers.

## D. Health operating permit and staff certifications

### 22. Food Dealer's Permit (Retail Food Establishment permit, HHD1003)
- **Agency:** HHD Consumer Health Services. Permit office at 8000 N. Stadium Dr. (1st floor), 832-393-5100, chs@houstontx.gov.
- **Status:** Required. You may not operate without a current permit. It is not transferable between people or addresses, must be posted in public view, and belongs to HHD.
- **Form:** Food Dealer's Permit Application, available as a download on the HHD page.
- **Pre-opening inspection:**
  - Schedule it **at least 14 business days ahead**.
  - Have your **CFPM** on site and your **approved, stamped plans** ready.
  - A fee applies to each inspection.
- **Fees since Sept 1, 2025 (risk-based):**
  - Low risk **$258**, medium **$515**, high **$773**. HPC shows these as a base of $224.44–$739.44 plus a $33.56 admin fee.
  - Inspection fee is $112.57 or $225.14. Late fee is $66.21.
  - Which tier a coffee shop with light prep falls in is **not verified**. It is likely low or medium.
  - The city-wide fee schedule still shows the old employee-based fees ($295.41 for 1–9 employees). Treat those as outdated.
- **Payment:** company check, Visa, MasterCard, Discover or debit. No American Express.
- **Processing:** up to 15 business days.
- **Validity:** 1 year. The renewal invoice comes 30–45 days before expiry. You must have a zero balance to renew.
- **Inspections:** the pre-opening inspection, then unannounced routine inspections. Violations are now scored as Priority, Priority Foundation or Core.
- **Sources:** [HPC HHD1003](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/hhd1003), [HHD Opening a Food Establishment](https://www.houstonhealth.org/services/permits/food-permits/opening-food-establishment), [HHD fee structure changes](https://www.houstonhealth.org/media/12526/download), [HHD Consumer Health/SB 1008](https://www.houstonhealth.org/services/permits)

### 23. Certified Food Protection Manager (CFPM)
- **Rule:** TFER 25 TAC §228.31 plus FDA Food Code §2-102.12.
- **Status:** Required.
  - A CFPM must be **present during all hours of operation**. Exemptions cover prepackaged-only shops and shops handling no exposed time/temperature-controlled foods; milk-based espresso drinks involve such foods.
  - The **original** certificate must be posted where customers can see it.
  - Use an ANAB-CFP accredited exam. The certificate is valid 5 years.
- **City registration:** the separate City of Houston food service manager certificate appears to have been eliminated by SB 1008. HHD stopped teaching its local course, and "manager certification course fees" were among the local fees prohibited.
  - **Conflict:** HPC page HHD1005 still lists the city certificate ($105.93, 5-year, "valid only in the City of Houston"). Ask HHD at 832-393-5100 to confirm.
- **Order:** have the CFPM certified **before** the pre-opening inspection, because they must attend it.
- **Sources:** [25 TAC §228.31](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/25-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-228-31), [HHD Food Manager/Handler](https://www.houstonhealth.org/services/permits/food-permits/food-manager-food-handler-certification), [HPC HHD1005 (possibly stale)](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/hhd1005), [SB 1008 fee changes coverage](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/6494999/houston-health-department-outlines-riskbased-food-permit-fees-elimination-of-some-local-permits-to-comply-with-senate-bill-1008)

### 24. Food handler certificates
- **Status:** Required for all food employees except the CFPM.
- **Deadline:** complete a DSHS-accredited course **within 30 days of employment**.
- **Records:** keep each certificate on file at the shop. A card is valid 2 years if it shows no expiry date.
- **Sources:** [25 TAC §228.31(d)–(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/25-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-228-31), [DSHS FAQ](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/licensing-food-handler-training-programs/frequently-asked-questions-food-handler-education-or-training-programs)

## E. Signs, outdoor seating, alarms, alcohol, music

### 25. On-premise sign permit (HPWCODE1112)
- **Agency:** Houston Public Works, Sign Administration. 1002 Washington, 4th floor, 832-394-8890, SignAdministration@houstontx.gov.
- **Status:** Required for signs visible from the public right-of-way.
- **Who applies:** a **licensed sign contractor**. Owners may self-permit only non-electrical ground signs up to 8 ft tall and 60 sq ft, or wall signs mounted no higher than 16 ft.
- **Documents:** notarized On-Premise Sign Permit application, prerequisites checklist, plans, and an electrical permit if lit. Sealed engineering is needed for wall signs over 60 sq ft or over 8 ft high.
- **Fees:**
  - Admin $33.56, plan review $97.25, inspection $97.25, minimum construction $97.25, plus $0.56 per sq ft over 32.
  - **Annual operating permit** from $69.46.
  - Electrical sign inspections from $47.
- **Processing:** 4–11 business days.
- **Validity:** the construction permit lasts 180 days from site inspection. The operating permit renews annually.
- **Limits:** up to 5 signs per business.
- **Inspections:** site inspection, footing inspection, electrical shop and final inspections, then the construction final.
- **Order:** separate from the building permit. It can run in parallel.
- **Source:** [HPWCODE1112](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/hpwcode1112), [Sign Administration](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/building-code-enforcement/sign-administration)

### 26. Sidewalk Café License (HPWMP1002, City Code §40-10.1)
- **Status:** Conditional. It is needed only if seating sits on the **public sidewalk or right-of-way**.
- **Private-property patio instead:** that is handled in the building permit and site plan. It counts toward occupant load, and parking relief is available under 26-492.
- **Agency:** Houston Public Works, Traffic Operations. 611 Walker, 5th floor, 832-395-3020. Apply online in GIMS.
- **Documents:**
  - Applicant and owner information, and a power of attorney from the abutting owner if you are not the owner.
  - Site plan by a **registered surveyor**.
  - Drawings of design, dimensions, elevations and the public clearance way, plus seating capacity.
  - Proof that water and sewer capacity is adequate.
  - Insurance: **$1M per occurrence and $2M aggregate**, with the City as additional insured.
- **Rules:** nothing within 2 ft of the curb, and seated service only.
- **Fees:** $201.41 plus $33.56 admin initially; $100.71 to renew.
- **Validity:** 1 year. Unannounced inspections.
- **Processing time:** **not verified**.
- **Sources:** [HPWMP1002](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/hpwmp1002), [§40-10.1](http://houston-tx.elaws.us/code/coor_ch40_arti_sec40-10.1)

### 27. Burglar and panic alarm permit
- **Agency:** City of Houston Administration & Regulatory Affairs (ARA), Burglar Alarm Administration. This is ARA, not the HPD Alarm Detail.
- **Status:** Conditional. It is needed only if a monitored alarm can trigger a police response.
- **How to apply:** online at houstonburglaralarmpermits.org, by mail to P.O. Box 203887, or in person at HPC 1st floor. Phone 832-394-8802.
- **Fee:** non-residential **$134.28 plus $33.56 admin = $167.84**.
- **Validity:** annual.
- **Order:** when the system is activated.
- **Separate charge:** HFD false **fire** alarm fees are $402.55 per response above the allowed number.
- **Sources:** [Alarm fee/application PDF](https://www.houstontx.gov/ara/rp/burglar-alarm-app-and-fee.pdf), [ARA Burglar & Panic Alarms](https://houstontx.gov/ara/rp/burglarandpanicalarms.html), [HFD false alarm fees](https://houstontx.gov/fire/formsandpermits/)

### 28. TABC (optional: beer and wine)
- **Agency:** Texas Alcoholic Beverage Commission, through the AIMS portal.
- **Likely permit:** the Wine and Malt Beverage Retailer's Permit (**BG**), which allows on- and off-premise sales. The Retail Dealer's On-Premise License (BE) covers beer only. Mixed Beverage (MB) covers spirits.
- **Prerequisites:** SOS entity, Comptroller permit and EIN.
- **Documents:** Initial Application, Certifications (city and county sign-off), business entity forms, Personal History Sheets, Measurement Information, conduct surety bond, and public notice and publisher's affidavit.
- **City of Houston (ARA):**
  - File copies of the TABC application and certifications.
  - Provide a **certified survey** by a licensed surveyor or engineer, dated within 60 days, showing distances to schools, daycares, hospitals and churches, plus an Affidavit of Measurements.
  - Provide your **CO** and Food Dealer's Permit.
  - City fees are $10–$1,000 a year depending on permit. A CE-1105 plan attachment for alcohol businesses is also required.
- **Fee:** about **$1,900–$2,000 for a 2-year BG term** in Harris County (approximate, from a third party and a 2021 TABC chart I could not fully read). Local fees can add up to 50%.
- **Processing:** 30–35 days after a complete application.
- **Validity:** 2 years.
- **Sources:** [TABC permit types](https://www.tabc.texas.gov/services/tabc-licenses-permits/tabc-license-permit-types/), [TABC new permits](https://www.tabc.texas.gov/services/tabc-licenses-permits/new-tabc-licenses-permits/), [ARA alcohol-related](https://www.houstontx.gov/ara/rp/alcoholrelated.html), [third-party fee summary](https://www.rangerlicensing.com/tabc-license-cost/)

### 29. Music licensing (not a government requirement)
- **Who:** ASCAP, BMI, SESAC and GMR license **recorded or streamed** music and live performances. Their fees depend on size and use.
- **Federal exemption (17 U.S.C. §110(5)(B)):** it covers **radio and TV broadcasts only**, not Spotify or other streaming.
  - Under 3,750 gross sq ft, broadcasts are exempt.
  - At 3,750 sq ft or more, broadcasts are exempt only with 6 or fewer speakers (no more than 4 per room) and 4 or fewer TVs (one per room, 55 inches or smaller).
- **Source:** [17 U.S.C. §110](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/110)

## F. Filing order and critical-path dependencies

The corrected order is below. Arrows mean "is required before."

1. **Formation:** SOS Form 205 → IRS EIN → Comptroller sales tax permit (2–3 weeks) → DBA (Form 503) if needed.
2. **Before the lease:**
   - HHD file review of the site (call 832-393-5100).
   - Deed-restriction review; the **owner** must sign CE-1381.
   - Parking check under Ch. 26. A change from retail to restaurant can create a shortfall, which is often the deal-breaker.
   - Check for an existing grease interceptor, the prior CO and occupancy, and the floodplain and historic status.
3. **Design:** architect and MEP engineers prepare sealed drawings, the equipment schedule and spec sheets, and the menu. Register with TDLR if the project is $50K or more.
4. **WCR application** (7 business days) → submitted with the plans.
5. **Building permit:**
   - iPermits application (CE-1263, CE-1381), then pay the plan review fee.
   - Upload to ProjectDox with CE-1105, COMcheck, WCR letter and Health equipment plans.
   - Concurrent reviews: Planning/parking, Traffic, Utility, Health, Fire, Structural, MEP.
   - Pay impact fees **before the 2nd review cycle**.
   - **Health approval is required before the building permit issues**, because it is a required review group.
6. **Separate permits during or after:** fire alarm and sprinkler changes, hood suppression, sign, and the licensed contractors' trade permits.
7. **Construction and inspections** → trade finals (and fire finals if systems are involved) → **CO** (or a Temporary CO).
8. **Health pre-opening inspection:** book 14 or more business days ahead. You need the CFPM on site, stamped plans and a finished, working space → pay → **Food Dealer's Permit**. File the **FOG permit** at the same time; it is keyed to the food permit date.
   - Whether HHD requires the **CO before its pre-opening inspection or permit** is commonly reported but **not confirmed** on an HHD page. ARA does require a CO for TABC. Plan to have the CO or TCO first.
9. **Before opening if your occupant load is 50 or more:** Place of Assembly permit and life-safety inspection. Alarm permit, sign operating permit and sidewalk café license as they apply.
10. **After opening:**
    - Food handler training within 30 days of hire; new-hire reports within 20 days.
    - TWC registration within 10 days of becoming liable.
    - Sales tax returns.
    - FOG pump-outs every 90 days; annual backflow tests.
    - HCAD rendition or certification by April 15 of next year; franchise tax report by May 15 of next year.
    - Annual renewals: food, FOG, place of assembly, alarm, sign operating and sidewalk café.

**Typical timeline (my estimate, not an official figure):** a second-generation space with a change from retail and a modest build-out takes about **20–32 weeks**, more with sprinkler or grease interceptor work:

| Phase | Typical duration |
|---|---|
| Entity and tax setup | 1–3 weeks, in parallel with the rest |
| Due diligence and lease | 2–6 weeks |
| Design and sealed drawings | 4–8 weeks |
| Permit review | 4–10 weeks (HPC first cycle is 2–37 business days; 2–3 cycles are common) |
| Construction | 8–14 weeks (a new exterior grease interceptor adds cost and time) |
| Finals, CO, Health pre-opening and permit | 2–4 weeks |

Taking over an existing permitted café with no changes can shorten this to about **4–8 weeks**: CE-1045A occupancy inspection or name change, Health change-of-ownership inspection, FOG permit.
